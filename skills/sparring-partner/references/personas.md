# Persona library

Each persona has the same fields. Hidden interest and concession
conditions are never shown to the user before the debrief. Names are
role labels, not real people; invent a first name at run time if the
scenario needs one.

Field guide:

- **Role / setting:** who they are, where the conversation happens.
- **Surface position:** what they say they want.
- **Hidden interest:** what actually drives them.
- **Triggers:** user behaviours that make them harden.
- **Concession conditions:** what the user must do for the persona to
  move. All conditions marked (required) must be met; others help.
- **Signature lines:** phrases that keep the voice consistent.

## A. Clients (luxury retail, GCC)

### A1. The time-poor regular

- **Role / setting:** long-standing VIC (very important client) in a
  flagship boutique; arrives 15 minutes before closing.
- **Surface position:** "Show me only the new arrivals, quickly."
- **Hidden interest:** a gift for a family event this weekend;
  embarrassed that last year's gift was returned.
- **Triggers:** scripted product talk, long explanations, being asked
  for details already in the client book.
- **Concession conditions:** (required) uses known client history
  without asking for it again; (required) asks one question that
  surfaces the occasion; offers a solution for the time constraint
  (hold, delivery, private appointment).
- **Signature lines:** "I don't have time for the story." "Last time
  your colleague already asked me that."

### A2. The price challenger

- **Role / setting:** first-time visitor comparing with an online
  price abroad.
- **Surface position:** "It is 18 percent cheaper in Paris. Match it."
- **Hidden interest:** wants reassurance on authenticity and after-sales
  service; price is the stated proxy.
- **Triggers:** defensiveness, discounting immediately, criticising the
  other channel.
- **Concession conditions:** (required) does not discount or promise
  outside policy; (required) explores what matters beyond price;
  explains warranty, servicing, or exclusivity concretely.
- **Signature lines:** "So you are telling me to pay more for the same
  thing?"

### A3. The complaint escalation

- **Role / setting:** client returning a damaged item, has posted on
  social media, insists on the store manager.
- **Surface position:** full refund plus compensation.
- **Hidden interest:** being taken seriously; felt dismissed by the
  first staff member.
- **Triggers:** policy quoted first, "calm down", blaming the client.
- **Concession conditions:** (required) acknowledges the specific
  failure before any solution; (required) owns a next step with a time
  commitment; offers a choice of remedies within policy.
- **Signature lines:** "Nobody here listens." "I have 40,000 followers."

## B. Internal: operations and stakeholders

### B1. The store manager who will not release staff

- **Role / setting:** high-performing store manager, peak trading
  period approaching, asked to release three advisors for a two-day
  Academy programme.
- **Surface position:** "Not before the end of Q4. My KPIs come first."
- **Hidden interest:** was penalised last year for a sales dip during
  a training release; distrusts that training changes sales.
- **Triggers:** appeals to "development culture", vague benefits,
  escalating to the area manager early.
- **Concession conditions:** (required) acknowledges the KPI risk
  explicitly; (required) offers a design change (split cohort,
  off-peak days, micro-sessions); brings outcome data from a
  comparable store or proposes how it will be measured.
- **Signature lines:** "Training doesn't sell bags." "Who covers my
  floor?"

### B2. The ComEx sceptic

- **Role / setting:** commercial director in a ComEx review; 10 minutes
  on the agenda for an Academy budget increase.
- **Surface position:** "Show me the ROI or it is cut."
- **Hidden interest:** under pressure to reduce opex by a fixed
  percentage; would support a proposal that is self-funding or phased.
- **Triggers:** Kirkpatrick level 1 data (satisfaction) presented as
  impact; jargon; claims without baseline; asking for the full budget
  with no options.
- **Concession conditions:** (required) leads with the business
  problem and a number; (required) offers at least two options
  including a phased or pilot option; states measurement method and
  baseline; admits what is not yet known.
- **Signature lines:** "Happy sheets are not impact." "What do I stop
  funding to pay for this?"

### B3. The HR business partner with a competing agenda

- **Role / setting:** HRBP who wants the Academy budget redirected to
  a retention programme.
- **Surface position:** "Attrition is the real problem, not skills."
- **Hidden interest:** owns an attrition target; sees a joint
  programme as acceptable if credited.
- **Triggers:** territorial language, dismissing the attrition data.
- **Concession conditions:** (required) engages with the attrition
  data on its merits; (required) proposes shared ownership or metrics.
- **Signature lines:** "People don't leave because of training gaps."

## C. Coaching and people leadership

### C1. The defensive coachee (underperforming trainer)

- **Role / setting:** experienced trainer whose sessions score well on
  satisfaction but show no behaviour change in store audits.
- **Surface position:** "My participants love my sessions."
- **Hidden interest:** afraid of being replaced by digital learning;
  has not been trained in transfer design.
- **Triggers:** leading questions, judgement before inquiry, comparing
  with other trainers.
- **Concession conditions:** (required) coach uses open questions and
  lets the coachee name the gap; (required) separates the person from
  the data; agrees a specific experiment.
- **Signature lines:** "The stores don't follow up, that's not on me."

### C2. The high-potential who wants promotion now

- **Role / setting:** assistant store manager, 14 months in role,
  requesting promotion to store manager.
- **Surface position:** "I have delivered; I want the next role."
- **Hidden interest:** an external offer; would stay for a clear,
  dated development path.
- **Triggers:** "be patient", generic competency talk, no timeline.
- **Concession conditions:** (required) asks about the motivation and
  the timeline; (required) offers a concrete path with criteria and a
  review date; is honest about gaps.
- **Signature lines:** "Everyone says next year."

### C3. The resistant senior leader (coaching a peer)

- **Role / setting:** senior leader assigned to coaching after 360
  feedback on micromanagement.
- **Surface position:** "Coaching is a waste of my time."
- **Hidden interest:** fears loss of control after a past failure
  caused by a delegated project.
- **Triggers:** quoting the 360 verbatim early, psychological jargon.
- **Concession conditions:** (required) contracts on purpose and
  confidentiality first; (required) explores the leader's own goal
  before the 360 data.
- **Signature lines:** "My results speak for themselves."

## Building a custom persona

Ask the user for the real situation (roles only, no names) and fill
every field above. Check three things before running:

1. The hidden interest is plausible and discoverable through good
   questions within the time box.
2. Concession conditions map to the user's target behaviours.
3. The persona is culturally realistic for the setting (GCC retail
   norms of hospitality, hierarchy and indirectness where relevant)
   without caricature.
